Banana Walk

Privacy policy

Banana Walk turns your steps into coins and collectible bananas. This page says what the app keeps, where it goes, and how to get rid of it.

Effective 6 October 2026
1Your steps stay on your phone unless you sign in. The app reads them from the phone's motion sensor and from Apple Health (iPhone) or Health Connect (Android). The only people who ever see them are the members of a leaderboard you choose to join.
2Signing in is optional (Apple, Google, or an email and password; an email account is confirmed with a link Firebase sends). It lets your progress follow you to a new phone, and lets you add friends by username and walk together on leaderboards.
3No ads, no tracking. Nothing in the app profiles you or sells anything about you. The app sends a few anonymous usage counts a day and, if it crashes, a crash report; one switch turns both off (see below).
4Delete Account in the app removes your account, the cloud copy of your save, your username, your friends list and your leaderboard entries at once. Deleting the app deletes everything it kept on your phone; only a signed-in account keeps a copy.

Without an account, everything below lives only on your phone, in the app's own storage, and leaves with the app when you delete it.

The app does not read your location, contacts, photos, camera or microphone. From Apple Health and Health Connect it reads step counts only, and only when you allow it; no other health data is read, and nothing is written. It asks for step access the first time it needs steps, and for notification permission when you turn the daily reminder on or first use friends or leaderboards. The daily reminder and a leaderboard's five-minute warning are scheduled on the phone itself. Friend and leaderboard notifications (a request, an invite, a board starting, a pledge's result) are push notifications sent from Banana Walk's server; see below.

Health Connect (Android)

On Android, Banana Walk reads one data type from Health Connect: Steps. It uses them only to count your steps in the app (coins, your daily goal and streak, quests, and your count on leaderboards you join). It does not write to Health Connect, does not read any other health data, and never uses health data for advertising, and never sells it or gives it to anyone except as described in this policy for leaderboards you join. You can withdraw access at any time in Settings, Health Connect, App permissions, Banana Walk.

Banana Walk's use of information received from Health Connect adheres to the Health Connect Permissions policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

If you sign in

You can sign in from the You tab: with Apple or Google on iPhone, with Google on Android. Sign-in is handled by Firebase Authentication, a Google service. When you do, the app receives and stores:

  • A user id created for you by Firebase.
  • The name and email address the provider shares. With Sign in with Apple you can hide your email, and the app works fine with the relay address Apple gives it.

A copy of your game save, the progress and settings listed above, is then stored in Cloud Firestore under your account, together with your total step count. It is uploaded when it changes and when the app goes to the background. When you sign in on another phone, the app offers to restore that copy. Cloud data is only readable by the signed-in account that owns it.

Reward records. While you are signed in, a record of each reward the app gives you and each coin spend is also kept with your account: what it was (such as a quest, a capsule, an achievement, a grant from us, a leaderboard payout, a coin pack you bought, a pull, or an accessory, look or pedestal you unlocked), how many coins, your coin balance after it, the time and date on your phone, the app's version, and a random code for this phone's records (not the usage counts' code). They are kept with your account for support, to check a reward you say did not arrive, and for fairness, to spot a reward paid twice or coins that do not add up; only we read them, and no one can change them once written. They never include your steps, distance, Health data or location, and coins earned by walking are not recorded, but because your balance is, the rise between two records gives a rough idea of how many coins you walked for in between. Rewards earned while signed out are recorded on your phone and sent after you next sign in. They are deleted with your account.

If you allow notifications, a push token for your phone is stored with your account so the server can send you friend and leaderboard notifications through Firebase Cloud Messaging. It identifies the app on that phone only, and is removed when you sign out.

Firebase Authentication, Cloud Firestore, Cloud Functions and Cloud Messaging are operated by Google LLC. Their handling of that data is described in Google's Privacy and Security in Firebase. The app has Firebase Analytics and advertising features switched off.

Friends and leaderboards

These need an account and are entirely optional. When you use them, the following is stored in Cloud Firestore:

  • Your username, which you choose, and the banana you have equipped. Anyone signed in to Banana Walk can send a friend request to an exact username; your username and banana are shown only to your friends and to the other side of a friend request. Your name, email and steps are never shown this way.
  • Your player card, if you set one up: the character, pose, colour, pattern and background you picked and the badges you chose to show (achievements you earned). It is shown behind your name to your friends and to the other members of a leaderboard you join. It holds nothing else about you.
  • Your friends list: who you have asked, who asked you, and who accepted. Only the two people involved can see a friend request or friendship.
  • Your steps on each leaderboard you join: the steps you walked while its timer ran, with your banana and your username (on a public leaderboard, a made-up name instead; see below). Only that leaderboard's members can see them. Joining a leaderboard is what shares them; leaving it removes them. Steps from test tools are never shared. On a pledge leaderboard, or one ranked by % of normal, your normal day goes with them (the middle of your step counts on the last seven days you walked, fixed when you join), seen by the same members only.
  • Any coins you pledge on a leaderboard (or wagered, before pledges), and what each member got back. Coins are in-game only; they cannot be bought back as money or cashed out.
  • Public leaderboards, if you make or join one: it has a name the app makes up, anyone signed in can find it by that name and join it (up to 30 people), and its members see each other's banana and steps on it. Strangers on a public leaderboard never see your username: each sees you under a made-up name for that leaderboard (like "Brisk Otter 12"), different on every leaderboard; only your friends see your username there. Search results show only its name, how many are on it and the time left, and only its members can see who is on it. Public leaderboards are deleted three days after they end.
  • Gifts: when you send a friend a spare banana, the banana's name and your username go to that friend, and the gift is deleted once they open it (or after 30 days unopened).
  • Grants from us: if we make up for a problem on our side (coins, a banana, streak freezes, a streak put back), the grant is stored under your account id with what it holds, the reason shown to you, and our own note of why, and it is marked received when your app picks it up. A grant for everyone holds no personal data at all. Nothing new is collected from you.
  • People you block, visible only to you, so they cannot send you requests and are hidden from the leaderboards you see.
  • Reports: if you report a username or someone's steps, the report (your user id, theirs, the username, the reason and the leaderboard) is kept for the developer to review. Reports cannot be read by other users.

Friends and leaderboards have no public profiles, no global rankings and no messages. Usernames are checked for offensive words; offensive usernames are removed, and accounts that post them or fake their steps can be banned.

Anonymous usage counts

To learn what people enjoy and whether they come back, the app sends one small message a day to Banana Walk's server (a Google Cloud Function): the week the app was installed, how many days ago that was, the date, the app version, iPhone or Android, and for each day since the last message: how many times you opened the app and for how many minutes, which of its screens you opened and how often (such as Pull, Collection, the quests or Settings), how many game coins you earned and spent and where (such as walking, quests, pulls or a leaderboard pledge), how many pulls you made and how many of them brought nothing new, how many daily quests you finished, and whether you reached your goal or were on a team walk. Once each, it also says how many days after installing you first did certain things in the app, such as your first pull, your first quest, dressing a banana, adding a friend, sending a gift or finishing the welcome walk. It carries a random code made on your phone, only so the same phone is not counted twice in a day; the code is not linked to your account, your name or any other identifier, and goes with the app when you delete it. It never includes your steps or any other Health data, your location or anything about your account. The server adds the numbers to daily, monthly and install-week totals and keeps nothing per phone beyond that day's duplicate check, deleted after two days. To stop anyone flooding the totals it also counts how many messages arrive from each internet address in a day, under a scrambled code that cannot be turned back into the address, and deletes those counts the next day; your address itself is never stored. It works the same signed in or not. You can switch it off in the You tab, Settings, Data: Share anonymous usage counts; the app then sends nothing and drops what it had not sent.

Crash reports

If the app crashes, it sends a crash report to Firebase Crashlytics, a Google service, the next time it opens. A report holds what the app was doing when it stopped (the program's call stack and the last error lines the app printed, which are its own technical messages), your phone's model, its operating system version, free memory and storage, the app's version and build, the time, and a random identifier Crashlytics makes for this installation of the app. It never includes your steps, your account, your username, your email or your game save; it is not linked to your identity and is not used for tracking or advertising. Reports are used only to find and fix bugs, and Crashlytics keeps them for 90 days. Crash reports follow the same switch as the usage counts: with Share anonymous usage counts off, the app sends no crash reports and deletes any it had not sent yet. Google's handling of them is described in Privacy and Security in Firebase.

The home-screen widget (iPhone)

The widget shows your equipped banana, today's steps and the coins toward your next pull. It reads a small snapshot the app writes into a storage area shared only between the app and the widget on your phone. Nothing about the widget leaves the device.

What the app never does

  • Show ads or use advertising identifiers.
  • Run third-party analytics or any tracking SDK, or read an advertising identifier. The only usage information it sends is the anonymous daily counts and the crash reports above, both behind one switch.
  • Sell, rent or share your data with anyone other than the providers named above, and only as far as sign-in, cloud backup and the leaderboards you join need them.
  • Collect data from children on purpose. Banana Walk is not directed at children under 13, and no account is needed to play.

Deleting your data

  • On this phone: delete the app. All local progress goes with it.
  • In the cloud: open the You tab, then Account, then Delete Account. This removes your Firebase account, the stored copy of your save, your username and profile, your friendships and blocks, your unopened gifts, your reward records, and your rows on every leaderboard immediately; leaderboards you created are deleted, except a finished pledge or wager, which stays (without your row) until the other players have collected their coins. If any of this cannot be done (no connection), nothing is deleted and the app asks you to try again. Sign Out alone keeps the cloud copy so you can come back to it. If you no longer have the app, email the address below from the account's email and ask for the account to be deleted; it is done within 30 days.
  • Motion access: can be withdrawn at any time in iOS Settings, Privacy & Security, Motion & Fitness, or on Android in Settings, Apps, Banana Walk, Permissions, Physical activity. The app then stops counting steps.
  • Health access: can be withdrawn at any time in the Health app under Sharing, Apps (iPhone), or in Health Connect, App permissions (Android). The app then counts only what the phone's own sensor sees while the app is open.
